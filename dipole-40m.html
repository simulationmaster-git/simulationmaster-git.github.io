Half-Wave Dipole
40m Band

A classic HF dipole for the 40m amateur band. Simple to build, easy to tune, and a reliable performer for both local and DX contacts.

Materials Used

  • ~21m of 1.5mm² stranded copper wire (cut to length after tuning)
  • 2× end insulators (repurposed from old antenna)
  • 1× centre insulator with SO-239 socket
  • Homebrew 1:1 current balun — 12 turns RG-58 on FT-240-43 toroid
  • 15m RG-58 feedline with PL-259 connectors
  • UV-resistant cable ties and weatherproofing tape

Build Notes

The formula for a half-wave dipole is simple: total length (metres) = 143 / frequency (MHz). For 7.150 MHz that gives 20.0m, so I cut each leg to 10.1m and trimmed down from there.

I started slightly long and used an antenna analyser to find the resonant frequency, then trimmed a few centimetres at a time from each end symmetrically until the SWR dipped at 7.150 MHz.

The balun is important — without it, the coax braid radiates and upsets the pattern. A simple choke balun wound on a toroid does the job well.

Lessons Learned

  • Cut long and trim — you can't add wire back once it's gone
  • Height matters more than perfection — even 5m up works well
  • An inverted-V configuration (centre high, ends lower) is easier to support and performs nearly as well
  • Always weatherproof the feedpoint connector — moisture causes SWR to drift
